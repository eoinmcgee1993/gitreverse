# Research dossier: Neuroglobe public offer

**Captured:** 2026-10-12 UTC  
**Primary URLs:** https://neuroglobe.store/ and https://neuroglobe.store/products/the-dopamine-protocol  
**Method:** publicly indexed page content and browser-readable page text. No purchase or login.

## Executive summary

Neuroglobe positions *The Dopamine Protocol* as a self-directed, 30-day digital programme that connects attention and motivation complaints to dopamine and autonomic nervous-system regulation. Its public offer bundles a long-form guide, daily cards, a one-page tracker and a continuation guide. The product page displayed **$47**, crossed against **$97**, with a stated **52% off**; the homepage also described a $50 saving. This is a page observation, not confirmation of checkout price, tax, currency handling or transaction completion.

Transferable commercial principles are the bundle architecture, specific audience pain points, concrete daily-use assets, a staged programme arc, a missed-day recovery message, and maintenance beyond the initial challenge. The riskiest strategic choice is presenting complex neuroscience as a confident consumer-facing mechanism, including receptor upregulation and broad claims that a month can “recalibrate” the reward system. The new product should compete on practical behaviour design and honest measurement, not on a neurochemical reset promise.

## Audience and positioning

### Directly observed
- The offer addresses people who report early phone checking, difficulty staying with a task, frequent stimulus-seeking, reduced enjoyment and trouble sustaining effort.
- It frames these difficulties as environmental and behavioural rather than moral weakness.
- It says the programme is self-directed, with written materials rather than video lessons or a community.
- It explicitly says it is not an ADHD treatment and should not replace medication, therapy or clinical care.
- It describes a daily time requirement of about 30–45 minutes.
- It gives a missed-day rule: do not double up, restart or punish yourself.

### Interpretation
The likely buyer is an adult who feels distracted or overstimulated, wants a clear structure, and prefers a private downloadable toolkit over a course platform. The page uses a combination of relief from shame, a simple mechanism, a short time horizon, a bundle, and a low-friction one-time purchase.

### Not verified
No analytics, conversion rates, customer interviews, actual sales volume, refund rate, customer outcomes or independent validation of the programme were available. The product's 4.8 rating and 11 reviews were displayed on the product page, but the underlying reviews and review-verification process were not audited.

## Trust signals and disclaimers

- Displayed rating: 4.8 from 11 ratings/reviews.
- References named in the homepage include Volkow et al. (2009), Wang et al. (2014), Porges (2009), and Šrámek et al. (2000), plus logos/images for journals/publishers.
- Product page includes About, Contact, Privacy and Terms links.
- Footer says the programme is educational, not medical advice or a substitute for diagnosis, treatment or licensed clinical care.
- The FAQ explicitly says the programme is not a treatment for ADHD and advises keeping clinicians involved.

The presence of journal logos or citations is a trust signal, not proof that each marketing inference follows from the original paper.

## Scientific claims that need care

The page makes or implies claims that a 30-day programme can recalibrate the brain's reward system; that dopamine receptor density recovers on a predictable timeline; that hyperpalatable food causes adaptations indistinguishable from substance dependence; and that cold exposure can produce a large sustained dopamine increase that shifts baseline. Those are not treated as verified simply because they appear alongside a citation. See the claim ledger for evidence assessment.
