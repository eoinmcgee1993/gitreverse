# Product specification: Attention Field Manual

**Working title:** *Attention Field Manual: 30 Days to Build Better Defaults*  
**Brand direction:** Digital Renaissance, with optional DegenDev creator signature.  
**Category:** educational self-management toolkit.  
**Product promise:** help the buyer observe attention friction, redesign cues and environments, practise small repeatable routines, and build a realistic maintenance plan. No claim of dopamine reset, clinical efficacy, or guaranteed transformation.

## Product requirements

### Target buyer
Adults who feel pulled between tasks, default to phone or browser checking, struggle to begin unappealing work, and want a practical, private, printable structure. The product is not positioned for diagnosing or treating ADHD, depression, anxiety, addiction or other conditions.

### Non-goals
- No diagnosis, treatment plan, medication advice, detox or supplement protocol.
- No claims about receptor density, neurotransmitter levels, nervous-system repair or fixed biological timelines.
- No promise that a habit will become automatic in 30 days.
- No requirement to buy an app, use a wearable, or share personal data.
- No guilt-based streaks or punishment for missed days.

## Original framework: SCOPE

1. **See it (Days 1–7):** notice triggers, contexts, task friction and attention switches without moral judgement.
2. **Cut friction (Days 8–14):** alter the environment, define starting actions and make distractions less automatic.
3. **Operate (Days 15–21):** practise focused blocks, implementation intentions, recovery breaks and realistic task sequencing.
4. **Preserve (Days 22–30):** review evidence, build fallback plans, anticipate disruptions and create a 60-day continuation.

SCOPE is a product framework, not a scientific scale or validated psychological model.

## Daily format

Each day contains:
- **Aim:** one sentence.
- **Practice:** 5–20 minutes, with a low-energy version.
- **Prompt:** one written reflection.
- **Measure:** one observable check, not a medical metric.
- **Fallback:** what to do when the planned practice is not possible.

## 30-day curriculum

### Phase 1: See it
1. Baseline without judgement: record three attention-friction moments.
2. Choose one target: define the behaviour you want to change in observable terms.
3. Trigger map: identify time, place, emotion and device cues.
4. Task friction audit: write the first physical action for one avoided task.
5. Attention budget: mark the three periods you want to protect.
6. Notification inventory: disable or batch one low-value alert.
7. Week review: note one pattern, one surprise and one adjustment.

### Phase 2: Cut friction
8. Phone parking: choose a repeatable place for the phone during one task.
9. Single-tab start: prepare one document or tool before beginning.
10. If-then plan: “If [cue], then I will [small action].”
11. Two-minute launch: begin with the smallest visible action.
12. Environment reset: arrange the workspace to make the target action easier.
13. Distraction capture: write interruptions on a side list instead of following them.
14. Week review: keep the changes that helped; remove the ones that added friction.

### Phase 3: Operate
15. One focus block: choose a realistic 10–25 minute block.
16. Define done: set a clear stopping condition before starting.
17. Break deliberately: choose a short, non-scroll break.
18. Task sequencing: put one demanding task before a chosen low-value default.
19. Restart practice: after interruption, write the next action and resume.
20. Energy-aware planning: match task difficulty to the time you tend to work best.
21. Week review: compare completed actions with planned actions, without scoring self-worth.

### Phase 4: Preserve
22. Friction audit 2.0: identify which distractions still slip through.
23. Recovery menu: list three small actions for low-energy days.
24. Missed-day plan: decide how to resume without doubling up or restarting.
25. Social boundary: write one polite sentence for protecting a focus period.
26. Weekly reset: schedule a 10-minute review at a stable time.
27. Disruption rehearsal: plan for travel, illness, deadlines or a poor night of sleep.
28. Keep / change / drop: choose one of each from the month.
29. Design the next 60 days: select one core habit and one backup behaviour.
30. Closeout: review what changed in observable behaviour and what remains difficult.

## Daily reflection prompts
Use one prompt per day aligned to the curriculum:
1. When did my attention feel easiest to direct today?
2. What one behaviour would make tomorrow slightly easier?
3. What cue preceded the switch I most regret?
4. What was the smallest useful next action?
5. Which time block did I protect, and why?
6. Which alert actually needed immediate attention?
7. What pattern did I notice without blaming myself?
8. What changed when my phone was out of reach?
9. What did I open first, and what did I intend to do?
10. What cue can I attach to the behaviour I want?
11. What did I do after the two-minute start?
12. Which environmental change removed the most friction?
13. Which interruption could wait?
14. What will I keep and what will I remove?
15. What made the focus block realistic?
16. How clearly did I define “done”?
17. Did my break restore me or pull me into another loop?
18. Which task benefited from being done first?
19. What helped me return after an interruption?
20. When did I have the best available energy?
21. What did my actions show, regardless of mood?
22. Which trigger still needs a better response?
23. What is the smallest acceptable version of the routine?
24. What does a non-punitive restart look like?
25. What boundary can I communicate clearly?
26. What is the simplest weekly review I will actually do?
27. What is my plan for a disrupted week?
28. What deserves to stay, change or disappear?
29. What one behaviour is worth repeating for 60 more days?
30. What evidence do I have, and what do I still not know?

## Deliverables
1. **Field Manual PDF**: introduction, scope and disclaimers, SCOPE framework, daily pages, examples, troubleshooting and maintenance.
2. **30 action cards**: each includes aim, practice, prompt, measurement and fallback; print and phone-friendly layouts.
3. **One-page tracker**: planned / attempted / completed / note columns. No punitive streak mechanic.
4. **60-day maintenance guide**: weekly reviews, drift signals, re-entry plans and adaptation rules.
5. **References and evidence notes**: each substantive claim linked to the relevant source and scope.

## Printable tracker design
Rows: Days 1–30. Columns: Date; chosen action; attempted?; completed?; friction/observation; next adjustment. Use checkboxes and brief notes. Include a note: missing a day is information, not failure. Do not include dopamine levels, receptor recovery, or diagnostic scores.

## Safety and accessibility
- State clearly that the programme is educational and is not medical advice, diagnosis or treatment.
- Encourage users with persistent distress, significant impairment or safety concerns to contact an appropriate clinician or local support service.
- Do not suggest stopping prescribed medication or replacing professional care.
- Make cold exposure, intense exercise, fasting and supplement use non-required; do not prescribe them.
- Offer low-energy alternatives and screen-reader-friendly, high-contrast, printable text.
- Avoid collecting sensitive personal information; the paper tracker can remain private.
